Lecture 4: Photosynthesis

BIO 101: Introduction to Plant Biology

1. What photosynthesis is

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It is the source of almost all the energy in food chains and of the oxygen in the atmosphere.

2. The overall equation

6 CO2 + 6 H2O → C6H12O6 + 6 O2 (light energy, chlorophyll)

  • Reactants: carbon dioxide (from the air, enters through stomata) and water (absorbed by the roots, carried up the xylem).
  • Products: glucose and oxygen. Oxygen is released as a by-product through the stomata.

3. Where it happens

  • Photosynthesis takes place in the chloroplasts, mostly in the palisade mesophyll cells of the leaves.
  • Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, a green pigment that absorbs mainly red and blue light and reflects green light.
  • Roots do not photosynthesise: they receive no light and have no chloroplasts. Roots absorb water and mineral ions.

4. The two stages

  • Light-dependent reactions (thylakoid membranes): light energy splits water (photolysis), releasing oxygen and producing ATP and NADPH.
  • Light-independent reactions / Calvin cycle (stroma): ATP and NADPH are used to fix carbon dioxide into glucose.

5. Limiting factors

The rate of photosynthesis is limited by whichever factor is in shortest supply:

  • Light intensity: rate rises with light until another factor becomes limiting.
  • Carbon dioxide concentration: more CO2 increases the rate up to a plateau.
  • Temperature: enzymes of the Calvin cycle work faster when warmer, but denature above about 40°C.

6. What the glucose is used for

  • Respiration, to release energy.
  • Converted to starch for storage, cellulose for cell walls, and proteins (with nitrate ions from the soil).
Key points for the exam: photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts in the leaves; chlorophyll absorbs light; carbon dioxide and water go in; glucose and oxygen come out; oxygen is released, not taken in.
